How it plays

What there is to do

Combat, loot, dungeons, the endgame, battlegrounds, group play and a life outside the fight.

Combat

Fast, readable fights

Click to move or use the keyboard, and fire skills from the action bar. Monsters come in packs with roles (chargers, casters, brutes, archers) and some roll affixes that make them faster, tougher or meaner. Bosses change phase as their life falls, call for help, and telegraph their biggest attacks on the floor so you can step out of the way.

Damage comes in physical, fire, cold, lightning and chaos, each with its own ailments: ignite, chill and freeze, shock, bleeding and poison. Armour, evasion, ward, block and resistances each answer a different threat.

  • Melee, projectiles, chains, novas, cones and targeted areas
  • Minions, totems, traps, auras, curses and warcries
  • Charges (Bulwark, Fervor, Insight), leech and life on hit

Loot

Items worth looking at

Drops roll a rarity, random affixes and sockets on one of 273 bases. Magic items carry up to two affixes, rares up to six; 37 unique items and 16 sets have fixed properties of their own. Item level decides the best affix tier a drop can roll.

Crafting uses shards rather than gold: a Tinker's Shard makes a normal item magic, a Sovereign Shard makes it rare, a Purging Shard wipes it clean, and essences reroll an item with a guaranteed property. Enchanters can imbue a crafted property, and a loot filter keeps the clutter down.

Each item's icon is rendered from its own 3D model, so the helm in your bags is the helm on your head, and a phial's icon drains as you use its charges. The tooltip shows the item's picture, its rarity and every property, with the tier of each affix beside it.

Windows sit in carved iron frames with gilt edges and the window's name on a title plate, and the title screen and action bar match. Buttons warm under the pointer and sink when pressed, tooltips fade in on a frame tinted toward the item's rarity, and skills, passive nodes and quests each get a card of their own. The interface art is the game's own, drawn at twice its size so it stays sharp.

Dungeons

Eight dungeons, a trial and two raids

Each dungeon is instanced for your group and built from a stone kit for its theme: the Hollow Crypt, the Rootdeep Warrens, the Drowned Grotto, the Cinder Forge, the Vault of the Last Lantern, the Moth-Eaten Loom, the Hall of the Warp, the Undertow, the Trial of the Lattice and two raids, the Spire of the Sundered Crown and the Drowned Basilica. Torches cast shadows through drifting haze, and doorways are arched in stone.

The eight story dungeons each take a shape of their own: the Hollow Crypt winds down past eleven tombs, Rootdeep Warrens opens into caverns, the Drowned Grotto runs in a ring of sea caves round the Cantor's flooded arena, the Cinder Forge is a run of pillared halls, the Vault of the Last Lantern, the second chapter's dungeon, is a run of warm slate halls beneath the Glass Scar, the Moth-Eaten Loom, the third chapter's, is a warren of halls and chambers hung with moth-eaten tapestries beneath the Heartroot, the Hall of the Warp, the fourth chapter's, is a run of icy vaults strung with glowing warp threads and webs of glass beneath the Warp Pillars, and the Undertow, the fifth chapter's, is a sea cave of black, barnacled rock whose shingle floors hold tide pools glowing with the warp's light. On the way down each has a mini-boss under a set piece of its own: Sexton Mordaunt by his open grave, Old Gnarltooth in a fallen hollow trunk, Captain Halloran Vane on the wrecked bow of the Morning Gull, Slagmaster Krag at his crucible, Ser Caddoc, the Wick-Knight, by a hearse of candles, Aldreth Silkhand at her distaff and great spindle, Vessrin, the Glass Brood-Queen, in a great web of glass strung across a ring of crystal spurs, and Old Hallam, the Drowned Keeper, at the Greywatch Light's lost lantern, washed up on a rock ledge with its Fresnel lens still turning. Every boss asks something of the group: marks to step away from or gather on, or a ward to break.

The bosses are dressed for who they are. Sexton Mordaunt digs in a slouch hat with his grave spade, Captain Vane wears a tricorn and barnacles and fights with a cutlass, Slagmaster Krag pushes brass goggles up over a red beard, the Twin Sirens have fins over their ears, Archdeacon Thessaly's mitre keeps its gold bands through his light, and Old Gnarltooth has gone grey, with old arrows still in his hide. Warden Ossric carries the lantern he calls the dead with, Choir-Mother Vesh stands under a halo of flame, and the Sundered King wears his crown broken in two. In the Vault, Ser Caddoc's great helm is crowned with burning candles, and Ilgrim, the last Lamplighter, wears a wide hat and a yoke of lanterns and fights with his lamplighter's pole. In the Loom, Aldreth wears a robe of undyed silk under a silk veil, glowing threads hanging from its hem. Boss rooms have set pieces that wake with their boss. Ossric's coffin lid grinds round and topples off; the Drowned Cantor's bell tolls; the forge vents steam and sparks; in the Spire the Rime Colossus rises from a frozen altar and Choir-Mother Vesh plays a burning organ before the Sundered King; beneath the Glass Scar the Last Lantern burns gold on its dais behind Ilgrim; under the Heartroot the Pale Moth wakes at the Weavers' Great Loom, its shuttle turning in cold moonlight; and beneath the Warp Pillars the Loomwright, a giant of ice-clear glass, stands at its frame of fifteen threads drawn up to a turning knot of light. Its glass, and the spiders', flashes at every facet and bends the hall seen through it. In the Undertow, Old Hallam wears an oilskin coat and a slouch hat crusted with barnacles and carries his lamp on a pole, and the Tideloom, a medusa the size of a cottage, spins the warp up out of its glowing pool.

Both raids are for up to ten. The Spire of the Sundered Crown is the level-30 climb to the Sundered King, past his Rime Colossus and Choir-Mother Vesh; the Drowned Basilica is a level-40 raid through twelve flooded halls of sea-worn stone and stained glass. Their bosses mark heroes to run out or to gather, raise wards to break before a channel ends, send adds crawling in to heal them and make their Defenders take turns, and every one has a berserk timer and scales to the size of the group. The Encounter Journal (X) explains each fight by role. Loot is personal, each encounter can be looted once a week, and each class has a tier set to collect in each raid.

Endgame

Beyond the Lattice Gate

When the Sundered King has fallen, Gatewarden Ysolde opens the Lattice Gate. Waystones dropped in the world open expeditions into 12 impossible places, each with its own boss, from the Ossuary of Whispers to the Unweaver itself.

Each area is built in a shape of its own: halls and chambers, a winding descent, a ring of rooms round an arena, a labyrinth, open caverns or pillared halls. Higher-tier waystones are harder and pay better, and each rolls modifiers that raise the rewards: more monster life, extra packs, curses on you, burning, chilled, shocked or desecrated ground, monsters that burst when they fall, or a second boss.

Every expedition boss has a fight of its own. The Bone Regent hides behind his honour guard, the Unweaver is warded by its pylons, and the Saltglass Overseer brings the roof down. Clearing expeditions earns atlas points for passives that shape what you find. The Season of Embers adds Lattice Rifts and titles for season characters.

Endgame

Warp Tears

Once the Loomwright is dealt with, the Lattice's oldest threads run out through the Gate, and where they snag the warp tears. About one expedition in five holds a Warp Tear: a rent hanging in the air of one of its rooms, a lens of the dark beyond edged with ragged warp-light, with loose threads frayed to the floor and splinters of glass turning about it.

Touch it and it opens. A ring of the warp spreads across the floor, and glass things come through inside it already hunting, stitched out of the air in threads of light: the Unspun, small half-spun spiders that swarm, with spiderlings, glass spiders and now and then a Warp-Weaver, some of them magic or rare. The tear stays open 16 seconds, and every one you unpick buys back three quarters of a second, so a group that kills fast keeps it open a long time while the packs come faster. A bar at the top right shows its time running down and the count. At 45 kills the Knot-Keeper comes through in a column of light, a glass spider two and a half times the size, carrying Warp Thread and two rare items.

When the time runs out the tear seals and what still stands unravels back into it. Warp Thread, a new currency, falls from the things that come through, from the Knot-Keeper and where the tear hung. Wenna Coldspindle weaves it into equipment from a new Weave tab in the crafting panel: 13 woven properties, from ward and frost resistance on armour to a glass edge on a weapon, speed on boots or a skill level on an amulet, one to a piece, re-woven whenever you like. Two atlas passives make tears more frequent and longer, and five achievements come with them.

Endgame

Tidewrack

Since the Undertow the warp goes out with every tide, and where it runs out past the Gate the sea goes after it. Once you have been down into the Undertow, about one expedition in five holds a Tide Bell: a ship's bell gone green under the sea, hung from a frame of driftwood over a cairn of tide-worn rocks, with kelp hanging from the beam and sea glass glowing among the stones.

Ring it and the sea comes in over every floor of the map. In 7 seconds it stands knee deep through every room and passage, holds at high water for 34 seconds, then drains away over 8. The water is a new shader laid over the floors: clear dark sea with the stones seen through it, bent by ripples, lit from beneath by the warp's light, with foam wherever a wall, a pillar or a leg stands up through it. Wading slows you a little, every step splashes, and medusae drift in on the tide near you, already hunting.

While it is in, 4 to 6 barnacled sea chests surface across the map, one to a room, holding gold, magic and rare items, Sea Glass and now and then a waystone. Whatever is not opened when the sea goes out sinks with it. At high water the Bloom Mother rises at the bell in a column of rose light, a medusa two and a half times the size, pink and gold, and she stays when the tide goes.

Sea Glass, a new currency, sets a tidemark in a waystone: its map holds a Tide Bell for certain, and more chests come in. Two atlas passives bring the bells more often and the high water longer, and five achievements come with them. A bar at the top right follows the tide, the chests opened and the Bloom Mother's coming.

Endgame

Hourglass runs

On Harrowford's square stands the Great Hourglass, a sandstone plinth with a brass frame on its pivot and a glass of gold sand. The sand runs for two minutes, the same moment for everyone, then the frame swings over and it runs again. Ysolde Marrowglass, the Horologist, keeps it and hands a sigil to any hero of level 35 or more. A sigil names a dungeon and a tier: the Hollow Crypt, Rootdeep Warrens, the Drowned Grotto, the Cinder Forge or the Vault of the Last Lantern.

Turn the sigil in the glass and its dungeon opens for you and your party at the sigil's tier, with companions to fill any empty places. Monsters are level 34 plus the tier, with 8% more life and damage for each tier past the second, compounding. After a ten-second count the sand runs: slay every boss and 85% of the enemy forces before the par time, from 7:00 in the Hollow Crypt to 15:00 in the Cinder Forge and the Vault. Each fall adds five seconds. Beat the par and the sigil rises a tier, within 80% of it two, within 60% three; run too slow and it drops one.

Each week brings three omens from an eight-week rotation: one from tier 2, a second from tier 4 and a third from tier 7. Hardened and Lordly toughen the trash or the bosses; Volatile, Kindred, Seething and Mending make packs burst, embolden each other, rage when hurt or heal from the fallen; Quaking, Grievous and Haunted crack the ground under you, open wounds that bleed until you heal, or raise rares as spectres. Each omen has a painted emblem and effects of its own in the dungeon.

A run ends with a cache of gold and items at the run's level. Your best run in each dungeon adds up to your Hourglass rating, each dungeon keeps a board of its ten best runs, and once a week the Horologist's Coffer holds an item for anyone who finished a run in time. A tracker under the minimap shows the clock against the par, the forces and the bosses.

World bosses

The Waking

Three world bosses of level 40 sleep in lairs out in the open world, and each wakes once an hour, twenty minutes after the last. Old Carapace, the Wreck-Bearer, heaves itself out of the sand of the Broken Shoals on the hour; Vorgrath, the Cinder Colossus, stands up out of the Cinder Crater at twenty past; and Elderhorn, the Moss Stag, walks into the Antlered Glade at twenty to. The whole island hears three minutes before one wakes. If nobody is fighting it after fifteen minutes, it goes back to sleep.

Each fights as a raid boss does: marks to run apart from or gather on, feeders that crawl in to heal it, a ward to break before its channel ends, Defenders taking turns and a berserk timer. Its life scales to the heroes and companions fighting it, so a party can take it on, and a lone hero can call companions to the fight at the lair. Everyone who fights shares the kill, and there is no tagging.

Each character's first kill of each boss in a week gives its spoils: a 30% chance at one of its two uniques, rising by 15% for each week it misses, or else a rare item and gold. Six uniques drop nowhere else, from the Wreck-Bearer's Hull to the Velvet Crown. The map shows how long until each lair wakes, and the Encounter Journal (X) keeps a page on each boss.

Vorgrath is fifty-three plates cut from scanned boulders over a core of molten rock. Old Carapace is the Reef Snapper grown old, carrying the stern castle of the wrecked Merrow's Due for a shell. Elderhorn is a giant elk whose antlers have grown into a crown of branches hung with moss.

Player against player

Battlegrounds and duels

The Shattered Hall sets the Lantern Compact against the Thorn Covenant, five a side, holding three hearthstones for renown. The Sunken Reliquary sets the Tidewardens against the Emberwatch: carry the other side's relic home while yours is safe. Queues are split into level brackets, and bots fill empty places so a battle can start.

Battles earn honour for a win, a loss or a draw, spent at Iselle Crane the Battlemaster on battle-forged gear and the Champion's Tabard. In the open world you can challenge anyone nearby to a duel.

Together

Parties, guilds and the queue

Group up for dungeons with the group finder, or join the dungeon queue as a Defender, Healer or Damage. If the queue cannot fill a group, named companions take the empty places, each with a role, gear and a voice. A party's leader can make it a raid of ten, and the finder's Raids tab fills one with two Defenders, two Healers and six Damage.

Guilds have ranks, a message of the day, arms of their own design shown on tabards and banners, a shared vault, and a hall in Gullhaven to rent. Players can trade directly, use the auction house at the Gullhaven Exchange, keep a stash, and send letters with items and gold through the post.

  • Chat, whispers, friends, ignore and emotes
  • Journaled trades with anti-duplication checks
  • Reconnection that keeps your party

Life in the world

Ride, fish, gather and craft

Buy a riding horse from Ivo Brack, Harrowford's stablemaster, and ride at a walk, canter or gallop. Escort Wila's wagon along the Merchant's Road to Millbrook while cutthroats prowl.

Fish the Saltreach sea, the clear shallows of Mirrorpool, Stillwater Tarn, Frostmere and the rivers, and cook the catch into food that keeps you Well Fed. Pick herbs by hand or take a Mining Pick to an ore vein. Each gather takes a few seconds and stops if you move or are hit. A vein holds several loads and now and then gives up a shard or some Lattice dust as well. What you gather feeds alchemy (phials and essences), smithing and enchanting.

Take a Skinning Knife to the beasts you kill for leather, and in the Peaks and the Wastes for rimefur and cinderhide. Ore is smelted into bars at the anvil outside every smithy. Smiths forge the bars into weapons and armour, and stitch leathers and hides into armour that always carries a matching property: cold resistance from rimefur, fire resistance from cinderhide.

Smiths also forge better tools: silversteel and embersteel picks, sickles for herbs and a flaying knife, each quicker and often good for one more. Find Herbs and Find Minerals show nodes on the minimap out to 240 metres, with arrows on the rim pointing to the nearest.

Professions in the wiki

The Saltreach Derby

A fishing contest every half hour

Every fish is weighed when you land it, from a herring of a few hundred grams to a Frostgill Pike of up to nine kilograms, and your heaviest of each kind is kept as a record. Nightcrawlers from the fishers bring bites sooner; Glimmer and Lantern Lures, which smiths forge from silversteel and embersteel, make the rarer fish of each water more likely, and a Lantern Lure brings bigger fish and more sunken coffers.

Every half hour Hesk Wadebury runs the Saltreach Derby off Gullhaven harbour. For twelve minutes the heaviest sea fish landed near the harbour leads, with five anglers of the town fishing it from the shore and a live board under the minimap. The winner takes 250 gold, the Saltreach Derby Trophy and two Lantern Lures, and the first time, Hesk's Prize Rod, on which fish bite a quarter sooner.

The Wardrobe

Look the way you want

Every piece you wear adds its look to a collection shared by all your characters. Odette Fairweave in Harrowford and Corin Dyewright in Gullhaven can restyle a worn item as any look you have collected, hide a piece altogether, or save whole outfits.

Ways to play

Alone, with friends, or on a server

All three run the same server code, so the game plays the same however you start it.

Single Player

The server runs inside the game. No connection or account server is needed, and your characters are saved locally.

Host

Start a game on your own machine and let friends join it over the network.

Dedicated server

A headless server for Windows or Linux that keeps the world running for everyone who connects.

Under the hood

How it is built

Aldervast is made in Godot 4.7 with GDScript. One authoritative server simulates the world: clients draw it, predict their own movement and send what they want to do. Every class, skill, item, monster, quest and dungeon is data rather than code, which is also how this site's wiki is generated.

Each release is checked by unit tests for the rules, simulated players on an in-process server, a smoke test that drives the real client, and a networked test with a dedicated server and two bot clients. Graphics settings, anti-aliasing among them, tune themselves on first play, and a built-in benchmark reports frame times.

Models, textures and sounds are CC0 (KayKit, Quaternius, Poly Haven, ambientCG, OpenGameArt contributors) and the fonts are under the Open Font License. Many models are built from those sources by scripts in the project, and every item icon is rendered from the game's own models the same way. See credits and licences.
