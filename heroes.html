Heroes

Six classes, five peoples

Every hero starts from a class and an ancestry, and grows through skills, gear and the passive web.

Classes

Choose where you begin

A class sets your starting attributes, skill, gear and place on the passive web. It does not lock you in: any class can socket any skill glyph, and the web lets a build drift towards neighbouring classes.

Ancestry and appearance

Make them yours

Choose one of five ancestries: the adaptable Hearthborn, the tall and long-lived Vaelin, the stout Durrak, the ember-marked Ashkin or the beast-blooded Wildborn. Each brings a small bonus, its own height and its own skin tones. Then pick a body, hair style and colours.

Armour shows what you wear. Helms, robes, cloaks, belts, shoulders and tabards each change the figure, and cloaks and robes move as you run and turn.

In motion

How heroes move

Every human in the world, heroes, companions, townsfolk, guards and human enemies alike, moves on a layered animation tree rather than one animation at a time. A walk that turns into a jog or a sprint picks up at the same point of the stride, so no step is skipped, and each stride is matched to the speed over the ground.

Weapons stay in hand at a run: a crossbow levelled in both hands, a two-handed axe, sword or staff carried across the body, a shield held up on the left arm. Attack or cast while running and the swing plays on the upper body while the legs run on; standing, it plays on the whole body, and the two blend as you stop or set off mid-swing.

Every blow is felt. A hit makes a figure flinch over whatever it is doing, chest and head knocked away from where the blow came, and a heavy blow knocks it back. Bodies lean into turns from the feet up, lean forward setting off and rock back pulling up, the head leads a turn, and townsfolk and guards turn their heads to a hero passing close by.

Feet find the ground. The animations are made on flat ground, so on a hillside the ground is read under each ankle: the hips sink as far as the lower foot needs, each knee bends to reach its own ground and a planted foot tips to lie along the slope. It works on open ground within about 32 metres of the camera; on bridges, house floors and in dungeons the animation's own legs stand. Eyes blink every few seconds, now and then twice, and glance about every second or two, and long hair swings on a spring: it trails behind a runner, swings out on a hard turn, lifts in the wind and settles.

Skin carries light under it, so shadow edges are soft and warm, with pores and a faint fuzz up close. Hair shines in bands along its strands, brows and lashes are darker than fair hair, eyes are wet and catch the light, and cloth has a soft sheen at grazing angles and a faint weave.

Growing stronger

Glyphs, runes and the passive web

Skill glyphs

Skills live in glyphs socketed into your gear. Red, green and blue sockets take strength, dexterity and intelligence glyphs. A glyph gains experience with you and grows stronger with its level; right-click a piece of gear and choose Unsocket all to take its glyphs out. There are 49 to find.

All skills

Support runes

Link a support rune to a glyph and it changes the skill: more projectiles, a wider area, chains, ailments, life leech. 36 runes, most with a cost to weigh against what they add.

All runes

The passive web

594 nodes in clusters around notables, with 14 keystones that change how a build works. You earn a point each level and from some quests, and can refund points.

The passive web

Passive art

A painting on every node

Every node of the web shows a painted emblem. Each of the 14 keystones and 49 notables has its own: the Glass Lantern a lantern burning in its cage, Stone Heart a heart of rock split by glowing cracks, Bloodbound a heart bound in chains, Wellspring a flask of mana, Hourglass Heart an hourglass. A cluster's small nodes share their cluster's emblem, each class's start has a crest, and the backbone's nodes carry a jewel for the attributes they give.

A node you have not taken waits greyed. It brightens once it is in reach or on the path you are planning, and shows in full colour once taken, and its card shows the painting beside what it gives. The emblems are modelled and lit in 3D, in enamel, gilt, steel, bone, glass and glowing stone.

A few keystones

Every keystone gives something big and takes something back.

Glass Lantern

Your spells blaze brighter, but your body is fragile.

  • 40% more Spell Damage
  • 30% less maximum Life

Stone Heart

Nothing moves you, including your own legs.

  • Cannot be Stunned
  • 30% more Armour
  • 8% less Movement Speed

Wildfire Pact

Steel becomes flame; flame remembers you.

  • 60% of Physical Damage Converted to Fire Damage
  • -15% Fire Resistance

Bloodbound

Skills cost Life instead of Mana.

  • Skills cost Life instead of Mana
  • 12% increased maximum Life

Hollow Crown

Your Ward doubles while your flesh withers.

  • 100% more maximum Ward
  • 40% less maximum Life

Thousand Cuts

You never strike critically, but every blow counts.

  • 100% less Critical Strike Chance
  • 30% more Damage
Specialisations

The Trial of the Lattice

At level 14, Elder Maren sends heroes to the Trial of the Lattice beneath the Nine Sisters. Its way in, the Sisters' Stair, is an old stone tomb-mouth just outside the ring of stones south-west of Harrowford, and the Group Finder's "Go to the Trial" takes you in from level 10. The Trial is walked alone. Completing it opens a specialisation: two per class, each with four notable passives that push the class in one direction. A Ravager can become a Bloodreaver who feeds on wounds or an Earthshaker who breaks the ground; an Arcanist a Stormcaller or a Pyreheart.
