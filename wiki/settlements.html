In the story

“You woke on the road with Lattice-light in your veins and no memory of the night before. Harrowford's elder wants to see you.”A Light in Harrowford, when you take it

“Go home to Harrowford, Vey says. Whatever you did down there, the woman who found you among the stones has waited twenty years to hear the end of it.”Lamplight, when you take it

“Weavers weave. We never once finish a thing: we cut it off the loom and hand it on, and it goes to the Cloth Guild to be finished, fulled and stretched and cropped and folded, and the guild has a Cutter-off to take it off the loom in the first place. Maud Kerf was the last of them. My grandmother's grandmother wouldn't sit down in a room with her. When the Lattice was first woven it went to the guild's Tenter Ground in the far south below Harrowford, to be hooked out on the frames to its width, and its edge has been on those hooks ever since. If she's cutting it off at this end, she'll be unhooking it at that one. Go and see. I'll follow when I've tied off what I can.”The Tenter Ground, when you take it

“I've an orchard south of the village, friend, twelve rows of apples and a row of pears at the bottom against the wind, and the three in the middle are older than Harrowford. My great-grandfather grafted them, and they've cropped every year since but the year the old king died. Since the Shears came out of the Pillars there's been Snips in the orchard. You'll know them, the cutting things that walk about on their points. They go up and down the rows cutting every tree off at the same height, the height of a man's hand held out, all the same, as if the trees were cloth being cut to a measure. And there's a man with them, or something wearing a cropper's apron, with a pair of shears as long as he is, who goes in front and marks the trees with chalk. The Pruner, the children call him. He's chalked my three old ones. Break the Snips, bring him down, and bind up the old trees' cuts with grafting-wax and sacking before the frost gets into them. I can't reach the high ones any more.”Coddle's Orchard, when you take it

In the story

“The briars creep closer to Lisswen every night, and the Bramblelings with them. The Covenant remembers those who help.”Covenant Duty: Thinning the Briars, when you take it

“The sigil leads east to the Thornwood and the Vaelin of Lisswen Hollow.”Into the Thornwood, when you take it

“The Vaelin let us cut the deadwood, and only the deadwood, and Lisswen gets its charcoal and nobody asks where a forest's fires come from. My family's burned in the clearing north of here for four generations. A clamp's a slow thing. You stack the wood like a beehive, cover it with turf and earth, light it, and then you sit with it six days and nights and never let it breathe too much or too little. Three nights ago the smoke went green. There were shapes walking in it, made of root, with the dark coming off their hands like more smoke, and they came at my brothers. We ran. The clamps have had nobody at them for three days. They'll have gone out, or they're about to burn through, and that's six weeks' wood. Clear the shades. Open the vents. You'll know it's right when the smoke goes blue.”The Charcoal Burners, when you take it

“The Honey Oak is older than Lisswen. We built Lisswen where it is because the oak was there. Its trunk is hollow from the roots to where the great boughs part, and the bees have lived in it longer than anyone has written down. We take a third of the comb at midsummer, never more, and the bees let us. Old Mossback takes all of it. He is an ogre so old the moss grows on his shoulders, and he has never been stung to death because he has never stood still long enough. This year he has brought the young ones and taught them, and they have torn the oak open to the heartwood. Drive them off. Bring him down, if you can. Then take three combs for Lisswen. A third. The bees will know if you take more.”The Honey Oak, when you take it

In the story

“The threads that run out through the Gate now, the ones the Loomwright pulled: I've followed them a little further every night. Last night one of them came back. Not through the Gate. In off the sea, at Saltreach, on the tide. The harbourmaster at Gullhaven has been sending word of lights in the water since the new moon. Ask her what her boats have seen, then go out to the Greywatch Light. Whoever keeps it will have seen the most.”Lights in the Water, when you take it

“Gullhaven's boats stopped going out a week back. The nets came up so full of those threads they glowed on the shingle, and the big ones, the lantern medusae, follow them in like gulls after a trawler. Cut the warp out of the nets they've left on the shore and put the lanterns out. Hesk Wadebury's the one who'll want to know: they're his nets.”Nets of Light, when you take it

“It's lit. Look at it: a wick of warp in the Greywatch lamp, and the sea lit to the horizon. They'll come to it now and drift there under the headland, and Gullhaven can fish. But I felt where the threads go, friend. Down. There's a cave under the Light, older than the Light, and the tide fills it twice a day. Something is down there, weaving them. And someone is with it. Someone holding a lamp.”A Wick for the Greywatch, when you hand it in

“The guild buried its own, friend, in its own ground, and it had its own woman to lay them out: to wash them and close their eyes and measure them for their winding-sheets, and sew the sheets to fit. Agnes Ellery. She measured my grandmother's grandmother for hers. Measured, never wrote it down: she never forgot a measure in her life, they said. The yardwands go south and west across the whole country to the Lych Field, on the downs above the Gullhaven road. If she's measuring the Lattice, I know what for. Go and see.”The Lych Field, when you take it

“There are stones round the Gate older than Emberwatch, older than anything. Hem stakes, Petra calls them: the Lattice was pegged to the land with them, like a sheet on a drying green. “Did you look at the cloth? Coming off the loom, between the two gates? I did, from the ridge, with my glass. It's Cinderreach, friend.
