Skill glyphs

Every skill a hero can socket and use.

Skills come from glyphs socketed into gear. A socketed glyph gains experience as you do and levels up with you, to level 20, and support runes in linked sockets change how it behaves. Every class can use any glyph; the colour of a socket decides which glyphs fit.

Each glyph is a painted stone of its own (1.14.43), its colour family set by its attribute: strength glyphs are reds (ruby, garnet, fire opal), dexterity greens (emerald, peridot, tourmaline) and intelligence blues (sapphire, aquamarine, iolite). The cut tells you the kind of skill: a melee skill is a hewn rough stone, a projectile a long pointed crystal, an area spell a round brilliant, a trap or totem a step cut, a curse or minion a rounded nugget, an aura or war-cry an oval brilliant, and a movement skill a smooth cabochon.

A glyph asks for a hero level: its skill's level, plus 2.6 for each level the glyph has gained (rounded down), so Piercing Shot, a level-1 skill, asks for level 11 once its glyph is level 5. It never levels faster than that, so a glyph you take out always goes back in. To take glyphs out, right-click the gear they are in, worn or in your bags, and choose Unsocket all: it takes out as many as your bags have room for. The crossbow skills and Shield Bash need that weapon in hand. Their glyphs go in any socket of their colour, on any piece, but until you hold the weapon your bar dims them and their tip says in red what they need.
