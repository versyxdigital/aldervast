World bosses

The Waking's four world bosses: their lairs, levels and hours, the fight, shared credit and weekly spoils.

The Waking: four world bosses sleep in lairs of their own out in the open world, and each wakes once an hour in turn. Old Carapace heaves the stern of a wreck up out of the sand, Vorgrath rises from a cold heap of stone, and Elderhorn walks in from the trees, all three at level 40; and since 1.14.83 Rimeback, the first at level 60, breaks up out of the drifts of the White Hollow in the Frostspire. Each fights with raid mechanics and scales to whoever comes; everyone who fights it shares the kill, and once a week each one gives its spoils, with a chance at one of its two uniques. Times are minutes past the hour, by the server's clock (UTC).

At Lanternfast a fifth comes (1.14.96): the Hush, Heart of the Long Night, rises in The Gloaming on every night of the feast, at the level of whoever brings a light, and gives its spoils once a day.

How it works6

The hour

Each boss wakes once an hour at its own minute: Old Carapace on the hour, Vorgrath at twenty past, Elderhorn at twenty to and Rimeback at 50 minutes past. The whole island hears three minutes before one wakes, in a notice and the raid warning across the screen, and again when it wakes and when it falls. If nobody is fighting it when its fifteen minutes are up, it goes back to sleep until its next hour.

Level
Old Carapace 40; Vorgrath 40; Elderhorn 40; Rimeback 60
Wakes

The fight

Each fights as a raid boss does, in three phases. From the start it marks heroes to run apart. Below 60% life it brings in feeders that crawl to it and heal it unless they are stopped; since 1.14.84 they set out from 22 metres at half their walking pace, so a group has about seven seconds to cut them down. It also marks one hero for the group to gather on. Below 25% it enrages and channels behind a ward that has to be broken before the channel ends, while it goes on marking heroes to spread. Its heaviest blow on a Defender stacks a rend, each stack 12% more damage taken, so Defenders take turns; when fewer than two Defenders fight it, the rend stops at three stacks (1.14.84). It goes berserk after seven minutes. Its marks, warnings and life bar reach everyone within 160 metres.

Shared credit

Everyone who fought it shares the kill: everyone on its threat list, and their party members within 90 metres when it falls. A companion's work counts for its hero. There is no tagging: two groups that never met both get it.

Who
Everyone on its threat list, and their party within 90 m
Companions
Count for their hero

Weekly spoils

Each character's first kill of each boss in a week earns its spoils, straight into your bags: one of the boss's two uniques at a 30% chance, 15% more for each time it has missed you, until one drops and the luck starts again. Otherwise the spoils are a rare item two levels above the boss and 80 gold plus 15 for each of your levels. Kill it again that week and you share the kill, but not the spoils. The week turns on Wednesday at 07:00 UTC, with the raids' lockouts.

Calling companions

A lone hero or a small party can call companions to the fight. Stand within 70 metres of a lair while its boss is awake and press Call companions to the fight under World Bosses in the Encounter Journal (X): up to four named companions come to fill your party to five, in the roles it is missing, at your level or the boss's, whichever is higher (40 at Old Carapace, 40 at Vorgrath, 40 at Elderhorn, 60 at Rimeback). They go once you are more than 220 metres from the lair, or when the boss falls.

Where
Within 70 m of the lair, while it is awake
How many
Up to 4, to fill a party of five

Keeping track

The map marks each lair with a horned diamond, its name and how long until it wakes (“Wakes in 12 min”), or a pulsing red ring and “Awake!” while its boss is up. The Encounter Journal (X) lists the four under World Bosses: where and when each wakes and its level, its lore and abilities by role, the uniques it can give, whether you have had this week's spoils, and when they reset.

Old Carapace, the Wreck-Bearer

Wakes at xx:00

A hermit crab of the Saltreach shallows that never stopped growing. When its last shell split it climbed into the stern of the Merrow's Due where she broke on the reef, and it has carried the wreck ever since, lanterns, rudder and all. It sleeps buried in the sand of the Broken Shoals, and when it wakes the sea runs out of the bay.

Elderhorn, the Moss Stag

The Vaelin say the first elk of the Thornwood was given its antlers by the Heartroot, and has never shed them since. Rimeback, the Bear of the Long Snow

Wakes at xx:50

The shepherds of the high shielings say a bear came over the pass the winter the Fell first opened, and lay down in the White Hollow to sleep until spring. Spring never came for it. The cold that blows out of the Fell settled in its fur, and it has slept under the snow ever since, waking hungry whenever the Long Snow falls. Each time it wakes it is bigger, and the ice along its back is thicker.

At the feasts1

The Hush, Heart of the Long Night

Lanternfast's nights

Every lantern lit at Lanternfast is lit against the dark, and the dark has a heart. The chandlers of Harrowford say it is all the lights that ever went out, gathered round the first lantern that failed: a cage of black iron with a dead wick in it, and smoke enough round it to fill the Gloaming. On the feast's nights it comes up out of the hollow where the fair's lights never reach, and it rises for whoever brings a light there, as tall as their courage. Bring it down and every lantern in the vale burns brighter till morning.

The Hush is Lanternfast's own world boss (1.14.96), not one of the Waking's four. On every night of the feast the dark gathers in The Gloaming, a hollow south-west of Harrowford, from the sky's dusk for about 18 minutes; the whole island hears when night falls there. Bring a light: when a hero of level 10 or more comes within 40 metres of the lair, it rises at the level of the heroes there, their average, from 10 to 60, so everyone of 10 and up can fight it. A hero under 10 is told it will not rise for them, and does not count. If everyone goes more than 90 metres off for a minute before the fight, it sinks again to rise for the next to come, and at dawn it goes down into the last of the dark unless someone is fighting it. A lone hero can call companions to the Gloaming as at the Waking's lairs.

It breathes the long night's cold on three of you (run out), drops the dark on one (stand together), and from 60% pours pools of night over the floor and calls the Unlit in out of the dark to mend it, 3% for each that reaches it. Below 25% it draws every light in the vale into its cage behind a ward of smoke: break the ward before its channel ends, or the night smothers everyone in the Gloaming. Its blows wrap a Defender in smoke, 12% more damage taken a stack, so Defenders take turns. It goes berserk after 7 minutes.

Its spoils come once a day, back at midnight UTC: 6 candles, a rare item two levels above its level and gold. Now and then a Kindled Unlit follows you home. Bringing it down earns the deed Every Light Comes Back and the title Dawnbringer, and the Gloaming lights up in gold over everyone who was there.
